# Platform Engineering — Real Interview Questions

> ~55 questions across 5 parts. Platform engineering interviews grade: whether you understand the platform as a **product** (it has users — the developers — and their experience is the feature), and whether you can design the **self-service** rails that let 100 engineers ship safely without a ticket.
> The one-line definition to open with: *"Platform engineering is productizing the developer experience — turning the toil of 'how do I deploy / observe / get access' into a paved road that's the default, not the exception."*

---

## Part 1 — Basic Level (what the role actually is)

**Q1. What is platform engineering? How is it different from DevOps and from "the infra team"?**
→ DevOps is a *practice* (culture + CI/CD + reliability habits). The "infra team" is a *cost center* that engineers *file tickets with*. **Platform engineering** is building an **Internal Developer Platform (IDP)** — a *product* with the *developers as users* — where deploying, observing, and getting access are **self-service** and the **safe path is the easy path**. The test: "does a new engineer stand up a service *in an afternoon, without a ticket*?" If yes → you have a platform. If they open a ticket → you have an infra team.

**Q2. What is an IDP / Internal Developer Platform? Name the core capabilities.**
→ The internal product developers use to build/deploy/operate. Core capabilities: (1) **Scaffold** (a "new service" button that gives you the code + CI + deploy + observability *for free*). (2) **Deploy** (self-service to staging/prod, with the guardrails built in). (3) **Observe** (dashboards/logs/traces auto-generated per service). (4) **Access** (envs, secrets, databases — self-service with *audit*). (5) **Cost** (your team's spend, visible). "The IDP is the *API* the company exposes to its engineers — its design is the *developer experience*."

**Q3. "Golden path" — what is it, and why does it matter more than the option to bypass it?**
→ The *recommended, pre-built* way to do something (deploy a service, add a database, enable a feature flag) — *opinionated*, *safe*, and *fast* by default. It matters because: (1) the *default* is what 80% of engineers take (people take the path of least resistance), so making the *safe* path the *least* resistance is the whole game. (2) It's where the *guardrails* live (the golden path *has* the SLO, the cost tag, the on-call hook — so taking it *means* being compliant). (3) The *escape hatch* (bypass) must *exist* (not everyone fits the mold) but be *louder* (a flag, a review, extra friction) — "escape hatches are for the 20%, and they should *sound an alarm*, not be silent."

**Q4. What is GitOps, and why is it the backbone of most modern platforms?**
→ The *desired state* of the cluster/infra lives in **git**; an agent (Argo CD / Flux) **reconciles** the live system to match git *continuously*. Why it's the backbone: (1) **git = the audit trail** (who changed what, when — the `git log` *is* the change history). (2) **drift detection** (someone `kubectl apply`'d by hand? the agent sees the drift and can revert — "the source of truth is *enforced*, not *honored*"). (3) **self-healing** (a node drifts, it's corrected). (4) **self-service** (a developer *opens a PR* to change their service — the *review* is the *approval*, no ticket). "GitOps turns *changes* into *pull requests* — and a PR is the most natural self-service + audit + review mechanism we have."

**Q5. What is policy-as-code? Give a concrete example.**
→ Expressing *governance rules* (security, cost, compliance) as **code that's checked automatically** (in CI / on admission), not as a *wiki* that people *might* read. Example: "no S3 bucket may be public" as an **OPA/Rego** policy that *fails the PR* if a manifest sets `publicAccessBlock` off — so the rule is *enforced* at *authoring time*, not discovered by a *security audit* 6 months later. The value: **compliance becomes a *build step*** — you *can't* merge a non-compliant change, so the *policy* is *free* (no auditor, no ticket). "Policy-as-code is the difference between 'please don't do X' (a wiki) and 'you *can't* do X' (a gate)."

**Q6. What's the difference between a PaaS (Heroku/Render) and an internal platform?**
→ A PaaS is a *managed, multi-tenant, external* product (you're a *customer*, it's *generic*). An internal platform is *your company's* paved road (it's a *product for your engineers*, it encodes *your* security/compliance/cost *policy*, and it's *tuned to your* services). The internal platform *uses* PaaS primitives (K8s, managed DBs) but *adds* the *company-specific* layer (your IdP, your cost model, your compliance, your *golden path*). "A PaaS solves 'deploy an app.' An internal platform solves 'deploy *our* app, *our* way, *compliant*, and *bill it to the right team*.'"

**Q7. What is "toil," and how does a platform specifically attack it?**
→ **Toil** = manual, repetitive, *unnecessary* operational work that *doesn't scale* with the product (hand-provisioning a DB, copy-pasting a dashboard, re-doing a deploy by hand). A platform attacks toil by **productizing the repeated action**: the 10th time someone "needs a Postgres in staging," the *right* answer is a *button* (a self-service "database" in the IDP), not a *ticket*. The *metric*: **% of actions that are self-service** (and the *time* to do a common task). "Toil is the *tax* on being a human; the platform's job is to *collect that tax* into a *feature* — so the 100th engineer doesn't pay it."

**Q8. What does "shift left" mean in a platform context?**
→ Move the *checks* (security, compliance, cost, *correctness*) to the *earliest* possible point — *at authoring / PR time*, not at *deploy* or *runtime* or *audit*. Platform-specific: a *new service* PR is *checked* for (the cost tag, the SLO, the on-call, the secret-scanning, the *dependency* scan) *before it merges* — so the *non-compliant* service *never gets born*. "On a platform, shift-left means the *guardrail* is in the *scaffold* — you *can't* create a service that *lacks* the SLO, because the *template* requires it."

**Q9. What is a "paved road" vs a "bypass"? How do you keep the paved road paved?**
→ **Paved road** = the *supported, maintained, fast* path (the golden path). **Bypass** = the *escape* for the 20% that don't fit (a *custom* deploy, a *raw* K8s manifest). Keeping it paved: (1) the paved road must be **faster** than the bypass (or everyone bypasses — *the #1 platform killer* is a paved road that's *slower* than doing it by hand). (2) the bypass must be **visible** (a dashboard of "who's bypassing, and why" — the *complaints* are the *roadmap*). (3) the paved road **evolves** (the *top* bypass becomes the *next* paved feature — you *pave* the demand). "A paved road dies when it's *slower* than the back road — the platform team's *north star* is that the *supported* way is the *fastest* way."

**Q10. How is a platform team different from a "product team"? (It *is* a product team.)**
→ Same discipline: the platform has **users** (developers), a **roadmap** (built from *their* complaints), **KPIs** (adoption, DORA, time-to-first-deploy), **support** (the "platform on-call"), and **release notes**. The *difference* is the *product* is *internal* and the *revenue* is *velocity* (the platform's "ARR" is the *engineer-hours saved* / the *faster DORA*). The *failure mode*: building it like an *infra project* (tickets, no users, no adoption) instead of a *product* (users, feedback, adoption). "If the platform team doesn't have *users*, a *roadmap*, and *adoption numbers*, it's a *helpdesk* — not a *platform*."

**Q11. What are the DORA metrics, and which does a platform most directly move?**
→ **DORA** = Deployment Frequency, Lead Time for Change, MTTR, Change Failure Rate. A platform *most directly* moves **Lead Time** and **Deployment Frequency** (self-service deploy + fast CI = *faster, more frequent* deploys) and **Change Failure Rate** (the *guardrails* in the golden path = *fewer* bad deploys). MTTR it moves *indirectly* (one-click rollback, observability). "A platform is a *DORA machine* — its whole reason to exist is to move these 4 numbers, so *measure them* per team, and *attribute* the movement to the *platform features*."

**Q12. What's a "backstage" / IDP tool, and what's the risk of adopting one off-the-shelf?**
→ **Backstage** (Spotify's open-source IDP) = a *catalog* + *scaffolder* + *scorecard* (the "service dashboard" + "create a service" + "is it healthy/compliant"). The *risk* of off-the-shelf: (1) **it's a *shell*** — the *value* is the *integrations* you *build* (the deploy, the cost, the *policy* — Backstage *calls* your systems, it *isn't* them; an *un-integrated* Backstage is a *pretty catalog that does nothing*). (2) **the *opinion*** (the *golden path* is *yours* to define — the tool doesn't give it to you). (3) **the *tax*** (you now *run* a platform *for* the platform). "Off-the-shelf gives you the *UI* and the *catalog*; it does *not* give you the *paved road*. The *paved road* is the *20% that's *yours* to build — and it's the *only* part that matters."

**Q13. What is a "scorecard" in an IDP? Why do you want one per service?**
→ A **scorecard** = a *per-service* dashboard of *quality/compliance* signals: SLO health, test coverage, *cost*, *dependency* age, *observability* completeness (does it have the SLO, the dashboard, the on-call?), *security* (secret-scanned, deps-scanned). Why: (1) it makes *quality* *visible* and *per-service* (the *platform* enforces a *floor*, the *scorecard* shows where each service *stands*). (2) it *drives* adoption (a team sees "your service is *missing* the SLO" → they *fix* it, *voluntarily*). (3) it's the *data* for the platform *roadmap* (the *most-common* missing signal is the *next feature*). "A scorecard turns *'is this service good?'* from a *vibe* into a *number* — and a *per-service* number, so *each team owns its own*."

**Q14. What does "self-service" *actually* require? (It's more than a button.)**
→ A button is the *tip*. Self-service *requires*: (1) **the *safe* default** (the button *can't* create something *insecure* — the guardrails are *in* the button). (2) **the *feedback*** (the user sees *what* was created, *where*, and *how to use it* — a "your DB is at X, the creds are in the vault, the dashboard is at Y"). (3) **the *reversal*** (a self-service *create* needs a self-service *delete* — or you get *orphaned* resources = a *cost* leak). (4) **the *audit*** (who created what, when — *automatic*, not a log someone *gathers*). "Self-service without *reversal* and *audit* is *self-*liability* — the platform's job is to make the *fast* action *also* the *safe*, *reversible*, *auditable* one."

**Q15. How do you measure whether the platform is *actually* being used (not just deployed)?**
→ **Adoption** metrics, not *deployment*: (1) **% of services on the golden path** (vs. bypass/raw). (2) **% of *actions* self-service** (deploys, DBs, envs — *done without a ticket*). (3) **Time-to-first-deploy** for a *new* service (the *onboarding* clock). (4) **The *ticket* rate** (the *fewer* "can you provision X" tickets, the *better* the platform — a *ticket* is a *platform bug*). (5) **DORA, *per team*** (the *outcome*). "A platform you *deployed* but that's *unused* is a *shelfware* — the *proof* it's working is that *engineers stop emailing the infra team*, and *DORA moves*."

## Part 2 — Real Job-Specific Questions (designing the platform)

**Q16. Design the "new service" experience (scaffold → first deploy). End to end.**
→ **Scaffold**: a "create service" form (name, language, *tier*) → generates a *repo* with the *golden template* (code, CI, a Dockerfile, the *K8s manifests*, the *observability* config, the *SLO* stub, the *cost tag*). **First deploy**: the PR *auto-detects* it's a new service → provisions the *namespace* + the *deploy* + the *dashboard* (from a *template*, keyed on the *metadata*) → the *golden path* is *on by default*. **The feedback**: "your service is live at X, the dashboard is at Y, the SLO is *unset* (fix it), the on-call is *unset* (fix it)." **The time target**: *under an hour* to a *deployed*, *observable* service — and *zero tickets*. "The 'new service' flow is the platform's *front door* — if it takes *days* and *3 tickets*, no one uses the platform, and the *bypass* wins. Make it *faster than the bypass*."

**Q17. How do you give a team a *database* (self-service) *safely*?**
→ A "request a database" in the IDP (type, *tier*, env, *team*). It *provisions* (a managed DB — RDS/Azure SQL — via *IaC*, *in the team's account/namespace*, with the *team's cost tag*). The *safety* built-in: (1) **non-prod** = *ephemeral* (auto-delete after N days — no *orphaned* dev DBs costing forever); (2) **prod** = *a *review* (a PR, not a button — prod data is *consequential*), *encryption* on, *backup* on, *private* (no public endpoint); (3) **the *credentials*** go to the *vault* (never in the *repo* — the app *reads* them from the vault at *runtime*). **The *reversal***: a "delete database" (with a *confirm* + a *backup* taken first). "Self-service *data* is the *hardest* self-service — because a *mistake* is *data loss*. So the *non-prod* is *frictionless* (and *ephemeral*), and the *prod* is *one review* (not a *ticket*)."

**Q18. Design *secrets management* for a platform (how do apps get secrets *without* humans copying them?).**
→ The principle: **secrets are *never* in git, *never* in the image, *never* typed by a human.** The flow: (1) a secret *lives* in the *vault* (Vault/SSM/Key Vault), *written* by *IaC* or a *rotated* process (not a human). (2) the app *references* the secret *by name* in its *config* (a `vault:secret/db-password` — a *pointer*, not the *value*). (3) at *deploy*, the *platform* injects it (a *K8s secret* from the vault, or an *env* set at *runtime*) — the *engineer* never *sees* the value. (4) **rotation** is *automatic* (the vault *rotates*, the app *picks up* the new value — no *redeploy*). (5) **audit** (who *read* the secret, when — the *vault log*). "The *test*: can an engineer *see* the secret's *value*? If *yes*, the platform *leaks*. The *right* design is *reference + injection + rotation* — the *human* touches the *name*, never the *value*."

**Q19. How do you make *observability* "free" for every service (so teams don't hand-build dashboards)?**
→ **Convention over configuration**: the platform *knows* a service's *identity* (name, team, env, *tier*) from its *metadata* → it *auto-generates* (1) a **dashboard** (from a *template*: the SLO, the latency p99, the error rate, the *saturation* — the *standard 4*, filled with the service's *metrics*), (2) **alerts** (a *default* SLO burn-rate alert — the *team* tunes the *SLO*, not the *plumbing*), (3) **tracing** (the *agent* is *in the base image* — a service *gets* tracing by *existing*). The *team's* job shrinks to "set the *SLO* target" — the *rest* is *there*. "Hand-built dashboards are *toil* and they *rot*. *Auto-generated* dashboards are *free* and they *stay consistent* — the platform's *observability* is a *template + metadata*, not a *per-service project*."

**Q20. How do you handle *environments* (dev/staging/prod) as a platform? (The #1 source of confusion.)**
→ Model *environments* as a *first-class* concept: (1) **dev** = *ephemeral* (a *per-PR* environment — spun up when you *open* the PR, *destroyed* when you *close* it — "preview environments"; this *kills* the shared-dev-staging fight). (2) **staging** = a *stable* "almost-prod" (for *integration* + *approval* — a *deploy gate*, not a *playground*). (3) **prod** = *the* prod (a *review*, a *canary*, a *rollback*). The *platform* makes the *promote* a *button* (dev → staging → prod) with the *gates* at each step. "The *environment* model is where *most* platforms *leak* — because everyone *means* something *different* by 'staging.' Define it *once*, *enforce* it in the *promote* flow, and *preview* environments *kill* the 'can I use staging to test my PR' problem."

**Q21. How do you *bill* / attribute *cloud cost* per team (and make it *useful*, not just a spreadsheet)?**
→ (1) **The *tag*** is *enforced* (a *policy*: no cost tag = no *resource* — so *every* resource *belongs* to a *team*). (2) **The *dashboard*** is *per team* (your *spend*, *trending*, *by service* — the *team* sees *its* number). (3) **The *unit*** (cost *per 1k requests* / *per user* — the *number* that *engineering* responds to, not just the *total*). (4) **The *anomaly*** (a *team's* spend *jumps 3x* → an *alert* to *that team*, not *finance*). (5) **The *idle*** (a *dev* env *running* at *weekend* → a *nudge* / an *auto-stop*). "Cost attribution *fails* when it's a *monthly* spreadsheet from *finance* — it *works* when it's a *real-time*, *per-team*, *unit-based* number that the *team owns*. The *platform* makes the *tag* *mandatory* and the *dashboard* *automatic*."

**Q22. How do you *gate* deploys (a *prod* deploy needs *approval*) *without* a *ticket*?**
→ The *gate* is *in the pipeline / the GitOps flow*, not a *human* ticket: (1) a *PR* to the *prod* branch *triggers* the *gate* (a *required review* from the *service owner* + a *policy check* — the SLO, the *cost*, the *security* scan). (2) *auto-merge* when *green + approved* (the *human* is the *reviewer*, not a *ticket-approver*). (3) a **canary** is *built-in* (the *prod* deploy *canaries* 5% first, *auto-rolls-back* on an *SLO burn* — the *gate* is *continuous*, not just *at* the deploy). (4) the *rollback* is *one-click* (the *safety net* that makes the *gate* *fast*). "A *ticket* is a *platform bug* — it means the *approval* isn't *in the flow*. The *right* gate is a *PR review* + a *policy* + a *canary*, so the *human* *reviews* (30s) and the *machine* *verifies* (the *SLO*), and *nobody* files a *ticket*."

**Q23. How do you keep the *platform* itself *reliable*? (The platform is a *dependency* for everyone.)**
→ The *platform* (the GitOps agent, the *scaffold* API, the *vault*, the *registry*) is a *critical dependency* — if *it* is down, *nobody* can *deploy*. So: (1) the platform has its *own* **SLO** (and it's *high* — it's *infrastructure for infrastructure*). (2) it's *observable* (a *platform* dashboard — the *agent's* health, the *reconcile* lag, the *API* latency). (3) it *degrades gracefully* (if the *GitOps agent* is down, the *clusters* keep *running* — the *platform* being down ≠ the *services* being down; *separate* the *control plane* from the *data plane*). (4) it has a *break-glass* (a *manual* deploy path, *documented*, for when the *platform* is the *thing that's broken*). "The *classic* platform *failure* is being *coupled* — the *platform* goes down and *nobody* can *deploy*. Design the *control plane* to be *down* *without* taking down the *services*, and have a *break-glass* that *doesn't go through the platform*."

**Q24. A team *bypasses* the platform (they `kubectl apply` a raw manifest, skip the golden path). What do you do?**
→ **First: *don't* just *block* it** (a *blocked* team *hates* the platform). (1) **Understand** (the *why* — the golden path *can't* do what they need? it's *slower*? they *didn't know*? the *bypass* is a *feature gap* or a *communication* gap?). (2) **Make it *visible*** (a *dashboard* of *bypasses* — "3 teams are on raw K8s, here's *what* they're doing" — the *data*, not the *shame*). (3) **Pave the gap** (if the *bypass* is a *real need*, *add it* to the golden path — the *bypass is the roadmap*). (4) **The *guardrail*** (a *policy* that *warns* on a raw apply — "this service isn't on the golden path, here's why that matters" — a *nudge*, not a *block*, *at first*). (5) **The *carrot*** (make the golden path *faster* — if the *supported* way is the *fastest*, the *bypass* *dies* naturally). "A *bypass* is *feedback* — it's a team telling you the *paved road* is *missing a turn*. The *platform* move is to *pave the turn* (and make it *faster*), not to *close the back road* (which just *angers* the *driver*)."

**Q25. How do you *onboard* a *new* engineer to the platform (their *first* service, *first* deploy)?**
→ The *onboarding* is a *product* (the *first-run experience*): (1) a **"hello service"** (a *pre-built* example they *deploy* in *< 30 min* — they *feel* the *platform* *working* before they *build* anything *real*). (2) a **"new service"** that's *guided* (the *scaffold* asks the *3* questions, does the *rest*). (3) a **scorecard** that *tells them what's missing* (after the first deploy, "your SLO is unset, your on-call is unset — here's how to fix them in 2 clicks"). (4) a **persona** (a *doc* / a *Slack* — "who do I *ask*?"). (5) a **time target** (a *new* engineer *deploys* a *service* in *their first day* — *not* their first *month*). "Onboarding is where the platform *wins or loses* a *user* for *life* — if the *first* deploy is a *3-day ticket*, they'll *never* trust it. Make the *first* deploy *the best* deploy."

**Q26. How do you *version* and *release* the *platform* itself? (It's a *product* — it has *releases*.)**
→ The platform is *versioned* (a *semver* — the *scaffold template*, the *golden path*, the *policy* are *versioned* artifacts): (1) a **changelog** (what *changed* in the *golden path* — "the *new* services now *include* X; the *old* ones are *unaffected*"). (2) a **canary for the *platform*** (a *new* version of the *golden path* is *opt-in* first, then *default* — you *don't* *yank the road* out from under *100 services* at once). (3) a **backward-compat** (an *existing* service *keeps working* when the *platform* *upgrades* — the *template* *evolves*, the *deployed* *manifests* are *stable*). (4) a **deprecation** (a *feature* is *sunset* with a *window* + a *migration* path, not a *yank*). "The *platform* is *infrastructure* — you *can't* *break 100 services* to *ship a feature*. So you *canary* the *road* (opt-in → default), you *version* the *template*, and you *deprecate* *loudly* — the *platform* releases are *migrations*, not *flips*."

**Q27. How do you *secure* the *platform* (it's the *most* privileged *thing* in the company)?**
→ The platform *can* do *anything* (deploy, read *secrets*, *provision* DBs) — so it's the *crown jewel*. (1) **least-privilege, *per action*** (the *scaffold* can *create* a *service*, but *not* *read* a *secret*; the *deploy* can *apply* a *manifest*, but *not* *delete* a *namespace* — *scoped* roles, not a *root* platform role). (2) **the *platform's* own *identity*** (a *service account* / a *workload identity* — *not* a *human's* *creds*; the *platform acts as itself*, *audited*). (3) **the *secrets*** (the platform *injects* secrets, but *never* *stores* a *copy* — it *reads* from the *vault*, *just-in-time*). (4) **the *audit*** (every *platform action* is *logged* — *who* triggered *what*, via *which* *flow*). (5) **the *break-glass*** (the *emergency* access is *separate*, *loud* (it *pages everyone*), and *time-boxed*). "A *compromised* platform is a *compromised company* — so the *platform* is *least-privilege* *per action*, *audited* *per call*, and its *emergency* path is the *loudest* thing in the *building*."

**Q28. Multi-tenant: 50 teams share the platform. How do you *isolate* them (so one team's *problem* isn't *everyone's*)?**
→ **Isolation at *every* layer**: (1) **compute** (a *namespace* / a *cell* per *team* — a *noisy* tenant *can't* *starve* the *others*; a *crash* is *contained*). (2) **data** (a *per-tenant* *DB* / a *tenant-scoped* *namespace* — a *team's* *data* leak *can't* *cross* *tenants*; *enforced in the* *DB*, not just the *app*). (3) **quota** (a *per-team* *limit* on *compute* / *cost* / *rate* — a *team* *can't* *burn* the *shared* *pool*). (4) **the *platform* itself** (the *control plane* is *multi-tenant-safe* — a *bug* in *team A's* *config* *can't* *break* *team B's* *deploy*). (5) **the *cost*** (per-tenant *attribution* — a *tenant's* *spend* is *visible* to *them*). "Multi-tenancy *fails* on the *noisy neighbor* — one *team's* *runaway* *job* *slows* *everyone*. The *platform* answer is *isolation* (a *cell* / a *namespace* / a *quota*) + *attribution* (so the *noisy* *tenant* *sees* *and* *pays* *for* *it*)."

## Part 3 — Advanced Level (platform architecture + the "product" depth)

**Q29. Design the *Kubernetes* platform for 100 services (the *architecture* question).**
→ (1) **The *cluster* model**: a *shared* cluster (or *a few*), with a **namespace per service** (isolation) + a **network policy** (default-deny between namespaces, *allow* the *declared* deps). (2) **The *base***: a *golden* *Deployment* *template* (the *resources*, the *probes*, the *labels*, the *cost tag* — the *service* *inherits*, the *team* *tunes*). (3) **The *ingress***: a *single* *gateway* (a *host/path* per *service*, *TLS* at the *edge*). (4) **The *config***: *config-as-code* (a *per-service* *config* in *git*, *applied* by *GitOps* — *not* a *dashboard*). (5) **The *secrets***: from the *vault* (a *per-namespace* *secret*, *injected* at *deploy*). (6) **The *observability***: a *base image* with the *agent* (tracing + metrics *by default*). (7) **The *scale***: an *HPA* (a *default*, *tuned* per service) + a *VPA* *recommendation* (a *suggestion*, not a *force*). (8) **The *safety***: a *ResourceQuota* per *namespace* (a *team* *can't* *eat* the *cluster*) + a *LimitRange* (a *pod* *can't* *eat* a *node*). "The *K8s platform* is a *template + a *policy* + a *vault* + a *GitOps* — the *service* is a *PR*, the *deploy* is a *merge*, and the *cluster* is *opinionated* so a *new* service *gets* *90%* of the *correctness* *for free*."

**Q30. How do you design *feature flags* as a *platform* capability (not a *per-service* hack)?**
→ A *central* flag *service* (a *store* + an *API* + a *UI*): (1) a **flag** is a *first-class object* (a *name*, an *owner*, an *expiry*, a *scope* — *%*, *user attribute*, *region*). (2) a **SDK** is *in the base image* (a service *reads* a flag with *one* call — *no per-service* *integration*). (3) the **UI** is *self-service* (a *team* *creates/toggles* a flag *without a deploy* — the *flag* is the *deploy*, for *behavior*). (4) the **expiry** is *enforced* (a flag *lives* 30 days, then it *alerts* its *owner* — *flag debt* is *visible*). (5) the **kill-switch** (a *global* flag that *turns off* a *risky* feature *in seconds* — the *incident* *valve*). "Feature flags as a *platform* turn *behavior* into a *runtime* *knob* — a *team* *changes* *what's live* *without a deploy*, and the *platform* *owns* the *plumbing* (the *store*, the *SDK*, the *expiry*) so no *service* *re-invents* it."

**Q31. Design a *contract* between the *platform* and a *service* (what the platform *guarantees*, what the service *provides*).**
→ The **SLA between platform and service** (the *paved road's* *terms*): **the platform *guarantees***: a *deploy* in *< N min*, *observability* *auto-generated*, *secrets* *injected*, a *rollback* in *< 2 min*, the *SLO* *measured*, the *cost* *attributed*, and the *guardrails* *applied*. **the service *provides***: a *standard* *manifest* (the *template*, *not* a *fork*), the *SLO target* (the *one* number the *team* *owns*), an *on-call* (a *human* behind the *service*), and the *cost tag* (the *team* that *owns* the *spend*). The *value*: it's a *contract* — the *platform* *can't* be asked to *hand-hold* (the *service* *meets* its *side*), and the *service* *can't* complain the *platform* *lacks* a *feature* (the *platform* *meets* its *side*). "A platform *without a contract* is a *helpdesk* (every *service* *negotiates* its *own* *deal*). A platform *with a contract* is a *product* (the *terms* are *fixed*, the *value* is *guaranteed*, and *both sides* know *what they get*)."

**Q32. How do you make the *platform* *multi-cloud* (or at least *cloud-agnostic*) *without* a *re-architecture* every *cloud* you *add*?**
→ **Abstract the *cloud-specific* layer, *expose* the *logical* one**: (1) the *golden path* is *cloud-agnostic* (a *service* is a *manifest* + a *SLO* + a *deploy* — *not* "an *EC2*" or "an *AKS pod*"). (2) the *IaC* is *Terraform* (the *same* *module* *deploys* to *AWS* *and* *Azure* — the *provider* is the *only* *cloud-specific* *bit*). (3) the *managed services* are *abstracted* (a *database* = "a *Postgres*" — the *platform* maps *Postgres* → *RDS* (AWS) / *Flexible Server* (Azure) *behind the scenes*). (4) the *observability* is *OTel* (the *traces/metrics* are *cloud-agnostic* — the *backend* is *swappable*). (5) the *secrets* are *vault-agnostic* (a *vault API*, not "SSM" or "Key Vault"). "Multi-cloud *fails* when the *golden path* is *cloud-specific* (a *service* is *hardcoded* to *EC2*). It *works* when the *platform* speaks *logical* resources (*a service*, *a DB*, *a secret*) and the *cloud* is an *implementation detail* — *Terraform* + *OTel* + a *vault API* are the *3 seams* that make it *possible*."

**Q33. How do you design *cost* into the *golden path* (so *cost-conscious* is the *default*, not a *review*)?**
→ **Cost is a *property of the service*, enforced at *birth***: (1) the **scaffold** asks for a *tier* (a *dev* / a *prod-small* / a *prod-large* — the *tier* *sets* the *size*, the *autoscale bounds*, the *DB size* — a *team* *picks* a *tier*, not a *raw* *CPU*). (2) the **quota** is *per tier* (a *dev service* *can't* *request* a *prod-large* *compute* — the *tier* *caps* the *spend*). (3) the **tag** is *mandatory* (the *cost* is *attributed* to the *team* *from minute 0*). (4) the **dashboard** is *per service* (a *team* sees *its* *spend* *in real time* — a *cost anomaly* *alerts the team*, not *finance*). (5) the **ephemeral** (a *dev* / a *preview* env *auto-stops* — the *idle cost* is *zero by default*). "Cost-consciousness *fails* as a *review* (a *human* *checks the bill* *monthly*). It *works* as a *default* (the *tier* *caps* the *spend*, the *tag* *attributes* it, the *ephemeral* *zeros* the *idle*) — so a *team* is *cost-aware* *without anyone asking*."

**Q34. How do you *evolve* the *golden path* when a *new* *best practice* lands (e.g., a *new* *observability* standard)?**
→ You *can't* *re-deploy 100 services* to *adopt* it — so you *migrate* *gradually*: (1) **opt-in first** (the *new* standard is *available* — a *service* *opts in* by *bumping* its *template version*; the *early* *adopts* *prove* it). (2) **the *scorecard* nudges** (the *scorecard* shows "your service is on *observability v1*; *v2* is available" — a *soft* *nudge*, *per service*, the *team* *migrates* *on its own schedule*). (3) **the *new* get *v2*** (a *newly scaffolded* service *gets* the *new* standard *by default* — the *old* *coexist* with the *new*). (4) **the *deprecation*** (after a *window*, *v1* is *deprecated* — a *loud* *alert* to the *stragglers*, a *migration* *guide*, and *finally* a *cutoff*). (5) the *template version* is *explicit* (a *service* *declares* its *template version* — so the *migration* is a *PR*, *reviewed*, *per service*, *not* a *big-bang*). "Evolution *fails* as a *big-bang* (you *can't* *stop 100 services* to *upgrade* the *road*). It *works* as a *versioned migration* (opt-in → *new* get it → *nudge* the *old* → *deprecate* *loudly*) — the *golden path* *evolves* the way *good APIs* *do*: *additively*, *versioned*, and *loud* about the *end*."

**Q35. How do you measure *developer experience* (it's *fuzzy* — make it *concrete*)?**
→ You *can't* measure *feelings* — you measure the *behaviors* that *feel* good: (1) **time-to-first-deploy** (a *new* service → *live*, in *minutes* — the *onboarding* *clock*). (2) **% self-service** (the *actions* done *without a ticket* — the *ticket rate* is the *anti-metric*). (3) **DORA** (the *outcome* — *frequency*, *lead time*, *MTTR*, *failure rate*, *per team*). (4) **the *escape-hatch rate*** (how often a *team* *bypasses* the *golden path* — a *high* rate = the *road* is *missing a turn*). (5) **a *pulse*** (a *quarterly* *survey* — *one* question: "on 1–5, how *easy* was your *last deploy*?" — the *subjective* *check* on the *objective* *numbers*). (6) **the *support load*** (the *platform on-call's* *tickets* — *fewer* = *better*). "Developer experience *isn't* a *vibe* — it's *time-to-first-deploy* + *% self-service* + *DORA* + a *pulse*. Measure the *behaviors*, and the *experience* is the *sum* — and if the *numbers* are *good* but the *pulse* is *bad*, you've *gamed* a *metric* (the *pulse* is the *reality check*)."

**Q36. How do you design *audit + compliance* as a *platform feature* (SOC 2 / ISO, *without* an *auditor* *chasing* *engineers*)?**
→ **Compliance is *generated*, not *gathered***: (1) **the *evidence* is *automatic*** (every *deploy* is a *PR* (a *review* + a *commit* + a *policy check*) → the *audit trail* is *git*; every *secret* access is a *vault log*; every *prod* *change* is a *GitOps* *reconcile* — the *evidence* is *born*, not *collected*). (2) the **policy-as-code** (the *controls* — *no public S3*, *encryption on*, *MFA* — are *enforced* in the *build*, so *compliance* is *continuous*, not *annual*). (3) the **scorecard** (a *per-service* *compliance* *signal* — *encrypted?*, *backed up?*, *has an SLO?* — the *auditor* *reads a dashboard*, not a *spreadsheet*). (4) the **immutable** (the *audit log* is *append-only* — a *tamper-evident* *record*). (5) the **report** (a *self-service* *compliance report* — a *team* *exports* *its* *evidence* *in 1 click*). "Compliance *as a platform feature* means the *evidence* is a *byproduct* of *working normally* (the *deploy is a PR*, the *secret is vaulted*, the *policy is a gate*) — so the *audit* is *reading a dashboard*, not *chasing 100 engineers* for *screenshots*."

**Q37. How do you *staff* and *organize* the *platform team* (it's a *product team* — but for *whom*)?**
→ The platform team is a **product team with the *developers as customers***: (1) **the *roles*** (a *platform engineer* (builds the *paved road*), a *DevEx* / *product* person (owns the *user* experience + the *roadmap*), and *SRE* (the *platform's own* *reliability* — the *road* must be *up*). (2) the **ratio** (start *small* — 2–3 *platform* engineers + 1 *DevEx*; the *team* is *leverage*, not a *center of excellence* that *does things for* *teams*). (3) the **model** (the platform *builds the road*; the *product teams* *drive* on it — the platform *doesn't* *run the services*, it *makes running them* *easy*). (4) the **KPI** (the *adoption* + the *DORA* of the *teams on the platform* — the *platform's success* is the *teams'* *velocity*). (5) the **trap** (becoming a *helpdesk* — *doing* the *deploy for* *teams* — instead of a *platform* — *making the deploy* *self-serve*). "The platform team's *job* is to *make itself* *less necessary* *per action* (a *self-serve* deploy means *fewer* *platform touches*) — the *KPI* is *adoption + velocity*, and the *failure mode* is *becoming the thing teams* *route around*."

**Q38. How do you handle *the platform's own dependencies* (the *registry*, the *vault*, the *GitOps agent*, the *K8s* itself)?**
→ The platform *depends on* *critical infrastructure* — so it *treats them* as *SRE does*: (1) **each has an SLO** (the *registry* is *99.9%*, the *vault* is *99.95%* (it's the *secrets*), the *GitOps agent* is *99.9%*). (2) **they're HA** (the *vault* is *HA* (a *replica*), the *registry* is *multi-replica*, the *GitOps agent* is *redundant* (a *primary* + a *hot standby* — a *dead agent* ≠ a *dead cluster*, the *cluster* keeps *running*). (3) **they're observable** (a *platform dependency* dashboard — the *registry latency*, the *vault* *health*, the *agent* *reconcile lag*). (4) **the break-glass** (if the *vault* is down, the *services* keep *running* (the *secrets are injected*, not *fetched live*) — the *dependency* being down ≠ the *service* being down). (5) **the upgrade** (the *K8s* / the *agent* *upgrade* is a *canary* — a *staging cluster* first, then *prod*). "The platform's *dependencies* are the *quiet* *SPOFs* — the *vault* down means *no new deploys* (even if *services* are *up*). So each gets an *SLO*, *HA*, *observability*, and a *degraded mode* (the *service* *survives* the *dependency's* *worst day*)."

**Q39. How do you *migrate* an *existing* service *onto* the platform (it's already running, *off-platform*)?**
→ **Adopt, don't *rebuild***: (1) **import** (the *running* service's *manifests* → *git* (the *current state* is the *starting point* — *zero* *downtime*, the *first diff* should be *near-empty*). (2) **conform** (the *manifest* is *mapped* to the *golden template* — the *labels*, the *probes*, the *cost tag*, the *SLO stub* — a *PR*, *reviewed*). (3) **connect** (the *service* *gets* the *platform's* *observability* (the *agent*), the *secrets* (the *vault*), the *deploy* (the *GitOps*) — *one* *capability* at a *time*, *verified* *between*). (4) **the contract** (the *service* *now* *meets* the *platform contract* (the *SLO*, the *on-call*, the *tag*) — the *scorecard* *goes green*). (5) **the order** (migrate a *stateless* service *first* (prove the *process*), the *data* / the *critical* service *last*). "Migration *fails* as a *rebuild* (you *stop the service* to *move it* — *downtime* + *risk*). It *works* as an *adoption* (the *running* service is *imported*, then *conformed*, one *capability* at a *time*, *verified* *between*) — the *service never stops*, and the *platform* *gains a* *user* *without a* *cutover*."

**Q40. How do you *prevent* the platform from becoming a *bottleneck* (the "platform team is the critical path")?**
→ The #1 platform *failure* is *becoming the thing* *teams wait on*. Prevention: (1) **self-service is the default** (the *90%* of *actions* are *self-serve* — a *team* *never* *waits* on the *platform* for the *common* thing). (2) **the exception path is fast** (the *10%* that *needs* the *platform* is a *SLA'd* request (a *ticket* with a *response time*), *not* an *open-ended* "when will it be done?"). (3) **the documentation is the product** (a *team* can *self-serve* *only if* it *knows how* — the *docs* are *tested* (a *new engineer* follows them *blindly*), *not* a *wiki* that *rots*). (4) **the escape hatch exists** (a *team* that *can't* wait *can* *bypass* (loudly) — the *platform* *doesn't* *block*, it *nudges*). (5) **the metric** (the *platform's response time* on *exceptions* is *measured* — a *bottleneck* is a *number*, and a *number* gets *fixed*). "A platform becomes a *bottleneck* when the *self-service* is *missing* (so *everything* is a *ticket*) or the *docs* are *dead* (so *self-service* is *impossible*). Keep the *90%* *self-serve*, the *docs* *live*, and the *10%* *SLA'd* — and the *platform* is a *road*, not a *toll booth*."

**Q41. How do you design the *platform's roadmap* (it's a product — where do the features *come from*)?**
→ The roadmap is *demand-driven*, not *platform-team-opinion-driven*: (1) **the bypass is the roadmap** (the *top* *bypasses* = the *most-wanted* *features* — "5 teams bypass for *X* → *X* is the *next paved road*"). (2) **the tickets** (the *platform's* *tickets* are *categorized* — the *top* *repeated* request is a *feature*). (3) **the DORA gap** (the *DORA metric* that's *worst* (a *slow* *lead time*) is the *next* *investment* (a *faster CI* / a *canary*)). (4) **the pulse** (the *survey* — "what *slowed you down* last *quarter*?" — the *subjective* *signal*). (5) **the prioritization** (a *RICE*-like score: *reach* (how many *teams*), *impact* (the *DORA* / *toil* *saved*), *confidence*, *effort* — the *platform* is a *product*, so it *prioritizes* like one). (6) **the anti-roadmap** (the *platform* *says no* to *one-off* *features* (a *single team's* *special* need is a *bypass*, not a *feature* — the *paved road* is for the *many*). "A platform roadmap built on *opinion* *misses* the *demand*; built on *bypasses + tickets + DORA + pulse*, it *paves* what the *teams actually* *need* — and *says no* to the *one-offs* that would *fragment* the *road*."

## Part 4 — Scenario-Based (real-time)

> Platform scenarios grade: do you think in **users (the developers)**, **trade-offs** (speed vs safety), and **adoption** (the road must be *taken*)? Use: *understand the user → make the safe path the easy path → measure adoption.*

**S1. A team says: "the platform's deploy is *slower* than my old `kubectl` script — I'm going back to the script." How do you respond (and fix it)?**
→ **First: *don't* argue — the complaint is *data*.** (1) **Understand** (the *why* — is the *golden path* *actually* slower? *measure it* (the *platform deploy* is *X min*, the *script* is *Y*). If the *platform* is *slower*, the *platform* is *broken* (a *paved road* must be *faster* than the *back road*). (2) **Find the slowness** (a *slow CI* stage? a *queue*? a *canary* that *waits* 10 min? a *review* that *blocks*? — the *deploy* is a *DAG*, find the *critical path*). (3) **Fix it** (cut the *critical path* — a *faster* build, a *shorter* canary *window* (with a *risk* note), a *parallel* stage). (4) **The *value* conversation** (the *script* is *fast* but it's *invisible* (no *audit*, no *rollback*, no *SLO*, no *cost tag*, no *on-call*) — the *platform* is *slightly* slower *but it's the one that* *survives the audit* and *rolls back in 2 min*). (5) **The *adoption* play** (make the *platform* *faster* (the *real* fix) *and* make the *bypass* *visible* (a *dashboard* of "who's on the *script*" — the *script* is a *known, measured* bypass, not a *silent* one). "A *faster* *script* is a *symptom* — the *disease* is that the *paved road* isn't *fast enough*. The *platform* move is to *make the road faster* (the *real* fix) and *make the bypass loud* (so it's a *known* *trade-off*, not a *silent* *drift*)."

**S2. The *GitOps agent* is down (the *clusters* are *running*, but *nobody can deploy*). It's a *Friday*. Go.**
→ **Stabilize**: the *services* are *up* (the *control plane* is down, the *data plane* is *fine* — *confirm* that *first*, and *say it* ("the *services* are *healthy*; *deploys* are *paused")). **Diagnose**: the *agent* (a *crash*? a *stuck reconcile*? a *git* *auth* *expired*? a *K8s API* *issue*?) — the *agent logs* + the *reconcile* *lag*. **Communicate**: the *incident channel* + a *status* ("*deploys* are *paused*; *services* are *unaffected*; *investigating the GitOps agent*; *next update 15 min*"). **Fix**: *restart* the *agent* (the *fastest* mitigation — a *stuck/crashed agent* is *usually* a *restart*), or *rotate* the *git auth* (if that's the *cause*). **The Friday rule**: if the *fix* is *risky* (a *config change* to the *agent*), *don't* do it on a *Friday* — *restart* (safe) now, *investigate* Monday. **Learn**: the *agent* has *no SLO* / *no HA* (a *single* *agent* is a *SPOF* for *all deploys*) → a *hot standby* + an *SLO* + a *break-glass* (a *manual deploy* path, *documented*, that *doesn't go through the agent*). "A *GitOps agent* down is a *control-plane* incident (the *services* are *fine*, the *deploys* are *paused*) — the *SRE* move is to *confirm the data plane is up* (the *reassurance*), *restart* (the *safe* fix), and *add the HA + break-glass* (the *systemic* fix) — *not* to *risky-debug* the *agent* on a *Friday*."

**S3. A *new* *best practice* (a *strict* *network policy*: default-deny *between* *namespaces*) would *break* 40 of 100 *services* (they have *undeclared* *dependencies*). How do you roll it out?**
→ **Don't *flip the switch* (it *breaks* 40 services).** (1) **Measure the gap** (a *dry-run* of the *policy* — *which* *services* *violate*, and *which* *undeclared deps* exist — the *data*, not the *fear*). (2) **Auto-declare the deps** (the *platform* *scans* the *traffic* (a *service mesh* / a *trace*) and *generates* the *declared* *deps* — the *40 services* *get their* *network policy* *auto-filled* from *observed traffic*, *reviewed* by the *team* (a *PR*, not a *ticket*). (3) **Roll out in tiers** (the *new* services get *default-deny* *immediately* (the *template*); the *existing* get *auto-declared* deps *first* (a *loose* policy that *matches* the *status quo*), then *tighten* over *quarters* (a *deprecation* of the *loose* policy). (4) **The *escape hatch*** (a *service* that *can't* conform gets an *explicit* *allow* + a *loud flag* + a *sunset* — *visible*, not *silent*). (5) **The *metric*** (the % of services on *default-deny* — a *north star* that *climbs* *quarter over quarter*). "A *strict policy* that *breaks 40 services* is *not a* *rollout, it's a* *big-bang. The platform move is to* *measure the gap*, *auto-declare* the *deps* (from *observed traffic*), *roll out in tiers* (*new* get it *now*, *existing* get *loose-then-tight*), and *climb a metric* — *security* *ratchets up* *without* a *cutover*."

**S4. The *platform's* *cost* has *tripled* this *quarter*, and *CFO* wants *it* *halved* in *2 quarters*. But the *platform* is *what makes the* *product teams' DORA* *good*. How do you handle the *tension*?**
→ **Don't *cut the platform* to *save money* — *reframe* the *cost** (1) **Attribute it** (the *platform's* cost = *shared infra* (the *K8s*, the *registry*, the *vault*) + *the teams' services* (the *namespaces*) — *separate* the *platform's own* spend from the *services it hosts* (the *CFO's* "platform cost" is *probably* *mostly the services*, not the *platform*). (2) **The *unit*** (the *platform's value* is *DORA* — *cost per deploy*, *cost per engineer-hour saved* — *reframe* from "the platform *costs* $X" to "the platform *saves* $Y in *toil* + *moves DORA*"). (3) **Cut the *waste*, not the *value*** (the *real* waste: *idle* *dev* envs, *oversized* *tiers*, *orphaned* *resources* — *cut those* (the *ephemeral*, the *right-sizing*, the *reaper*) — *not* the *HA* / the *SLO* / the *observability* (that's the *value*). (4) **The *committed use*** (a *RI* / a *savings plan* on the *stable baseline* — the *biggest* *zero-risk* win). (5) **The *refusal*** (say *no* to cutting the *DR* / the *observability* / the *vault HA* — "that's the *insurance*; cutting it *saves* $X *now* and *costs* $10X in the *next incident*"). "The *tension* is *cost vs. value* — and the *platform's* answer is to *attribute* the cost (*separate* the *platform* from the *services*), *reframe* it as *DORA* (*value*), *cut the waste* (the *idle*, the *oversized*), and *refuse* to cut the *insurance* (the *HA*, the *observability*). You *halve the waste*, you *don't* halve the *platform*."

**S5. A *product team* wants a *feature the platform doesn't have* (a *custom* *service mesh* config), and they *threaten* to *build it themselves* (a *bypass*). What do you do?**
→ **This is the *adoption* *moment** (1) **Understand the *need*** (the *why* — is it a *real* *requirement* (a *latency* need, a *mTLS* need) or a *nice-to-have*? a *custom mesh config* is usually a *symptom* (they *want* *m* to do *X* that the *golden path* doesn't expose). (2) **The *options***: (a) *the platform adds it* (a *first-class* feature — *if* it's a *common* need (3+ teams) → it's a *roadmap item*, with a *date*). (b) *the escape hatch* (a *documented*, *loud*, *self-serve* way to do it *outside* the golden path (a *flag* + a *review* + a *cost*) — *they* *build it*, but it's *visible* + *audited* + *sunset-able*). (c) *a partnership* (the *platform* + the *team* *build it together* — the *team* *knows the need*, the *platform* *knows the architecture*; the *feature* lands in the *road* (for *all* teams), not in *their* *fork*). (3) **The *decision*** (if it's a *one-off* → (b) the *escape hatch* (it's *their* *cost*, it's *visible*); if it's *common* → (a)/(c) (it's the *roadmap*). (4) **The *anti-pattern*** (the *platform* *says no* and the *team* *builds it in the dark* (a *silent bypass* that *rots*) — that's the *worst* outcome). "A *team* that *threatens to bypass* is *telling you the road is missing a turn. The platform move is to* *understand the need*, *offer the 3 options* (*add it*, *escape hatch*, *co-build*), and *make the bypass loud* (if they *build it*, it's *visible* + *audited* + *sunset-able*) — *never* a *silent fork*."

**S6. The *platform* *shipped a change* (a *new* *base image*) that *broke* 12 services (a *dependency* *version* bump). It's *your* *platform's* *fault*. Go.**
→ **This is a *platform incident* (the road broke under 12 drivers).** (1) **Stabilize**: *roll back the base image* (the *platform* change is *versioned* — a *rollback* to the *previous* image, *across all 12 services*, *automated* (a *GitOps* *revert*) — *fast*, because the *platform change* is *one* *artifact*, not 12). (2) **Communicate**: the *incident channel* + the *12 teams* ("the *new base image* broke *your service*; we've *rolled it back*; *here's the* *why* + the *timeline* for a *fix*"). (3) **Diagnose**: the *dependency* that *broke* (a *transitive* dep? a *breaking* *version*? a *CVE* *patch* that *changed* behavior?) — the *base image* is *your artifact*, so the *debug* is *yours*. (4) **Fix + re-release**: the *fixed image* is a *canary* (1 *service* → *verify* → *all 12*), *not* a *big-bang* (the *first* *release* was a *big-bang* — *that's the process gap*). (5) **The systemic fix**: the *base image* is *versioned* + *canaried* (a *new* image is *opt-in* → *default*, *not* a *flip*), and *tested* against a *canary service* before it *touches 12*. **Own it** (it's *your* *fault* — *say so*, *fix the process*, *don't* *blame the teams*). "A *platform change* that breaks 12 services is a *SPOF in the road itself* — the *fix* is a *versioned + canaried base image* (the *new* image is *opt-in*, *tested* on 1 service, *then* *default*), and the *incident* is *owned* (it's *your* *road* that broke, so you *roll it back*, you *tell the 12 teams*, and you *fix the process* so the *next* image is *canaried*, not *flipped*)."

**S7. You're asked to *build the platform from scratch* for a *50-engineer* company (no platform today, everything is *tickets*). 6-month plan.**
→ **You build *leverage*, not a *monument*** (1) **Month 1: the *one* paved road** (the *new service* flow — a *scaffold* + a *deploy* + a *dashboard* — the *single* *most-repeated* *action* (a *new service* / a *deploy*), made *self-serve*. *One* road, *done well*, *beats* 10 half-built features). (2) **Month 2: the *guardrails*** (the *cost tag* (mandatory), the *SLO stub* (every service *gets* an SLO), the *policy-as-code* (the *3* highest-risk rules — *no public*, *encryption on*, *MFA* — *enforced in the build*). (3) **Month 3: *observability free*** (the *auto-dashboard* + the *default alert* — a *service* *gets* observed by *existing*). (4) **Month 4: *secrets*** (the *vault* + the *injection* — a *service* *gets* secrets *without a human*). (5) **Month 5: *the scorecard + the bypass dashboard*** (make *quality* *visible* + make the *bypass* *loud* — the *data* for the *roadmap*). (6) **Month 6: *the DORA + the adoption*** (measure *DORA* per team + the *adoption* (% self-service) — the *proof* the platform *works*, and the *case* for the *next* investment). **The philosophy**: *one road, done well, adopted by all 50* — *not* a *grand platform* that *nobody uses*. "A *from-scratch* platform *dies* as a *big-bang* (a *grand* *platform*, *18 months*, *nobody on it*). It *lives* as *leverage* — *one paved road* (the *new service* flow), *done in a month*, *adopted by all 50*, *then* the *guardrails*, *then* the *observability* — *each* a *self-serve* win, *each* *measured* in *adoption* + *DORA*. The *6-month* *goal* is *50 engineers* *who stopped* *emailing infra*."

**S8. The *platform's* *on-call* is getting *paged for things that aren't the platform's fault* (a *service's* *bug*, a *team's* *misconfig*). The *platform on-call* is *burning out*. Fix it.**
→ **The platform on-call is *catching* the *teams'* *problems* — because the *boundary* is *unclear*.** (1) **Triage the pages** (for *each* page in the *last month*: was it *the platform* (the *road* is *broken*) or *the service* (a *team's* *code* / *config*)? — the *data*). (2) **The *boundary*** (a *documented* *contract*: the *platform* owns the *road* (the *deploy*, the *registry*, the *GitOps*, the *vault*); the *team* owns the *car* (the *service's* *code*, its *config*, its *SLO*). A *service bug* is *the team's page*, not the *platform's*. (3) **Re-route** (a *service bug* page *goes to the service's on-call* (the *team* that *owns* it) — the *platform* is *consulted*, not *paged*). (4) **The *self-diagnose*** (the *platform* makes the *service's* *problem* *self-diagnosable* (a *scorecard*, a *runbook*, a *clear* "this is a *service* issue, here's *where* to look") — so a *team* *resolves its own* *bug* *without paging the platform*). (5) **The *metric*** (the *platform on-call's* *page rate* for *non-platform* issues → *0* (they *re-route* to the *team*), and the *team's* *self-resolution rate* → *up*). "The *platform on-call* burning out on *service bugs* means the *boundary* is *unclear* (the *road* and the *car* are *mixed*). The fix is a *documented contract* (the *platform* owns the *road*, the *team* owns the *car*), *re-routing* the *service pages* to the *team*, and *making the service's problem self-diagnosable* (so the *team* resolves it *without the platform*). The *metric*: the *platform's non-platform pages → 0*."

**S9. A *security* team wants to *block all* *prod deploys* until *every* service passes a *new* *scan* (a *strict* *SBOM* check). 80 of 100 services will *fail* it *tomorrow*. The *product* has a *launch* in *3 days*. Go.**
→ **This is a *security* vs *velocity* collision — and it's a *business* decision, made *legible*.** (1) **Quantify** (the *80 failing* — *why*? (a *new dep*? an *old* *vuln*? a *false positive*?) and the *risk* (a *critical* CVE, or a *low-severity* *noise*? — the *severity* decides the *urgency*). (2) **The *options***: (a) *block all* (the *launch* is *at risk* — the *80 services* can't *deploy* in *3 days*; the *business* owns that). (b) *block only the *critical* CVEs* (the *high-severity* ones — the *real* *risk* — and *allow* the *low-severity* with a *ticket* + a *deadline* (a *risk-accepted*, *time-boxed* exception). (c) *a *canary* (the *launch* deploys the *few* services that *pass*, the *rest* are *excluded* from the *launch scope*). (3) **Make it a *business* decision** (the *SRE* / *platform* *presents the number* ("80 services, *X* *critical* CVEs, *Y* *low-severity*; the *launch* needs *N* services"), the *security* + the *product* *decide* (a *risk-accepted* exception for the *low-severity*, a *block* for the *critical*) — *documented*, *time-boxed*, *with an owner*. (4) **The *systemic fix*** (the *SBOM check* is *shift-left* (a *PR gate*, so a *new* *vuln* is *caught at authoring*, not a *big-bang* *block*), and the *80 failing* get a *remediation* *sprint* (a *known*, *tracked* debt, not a *surprise*). "A *security block* that *kills a launch* is a *business decision* dressed as a *technical one*. The *platform* move is to *quantify* the risk (*critical* vs *low-severity*), *offer the 3 options* (block all / block critical / canary), and *make it a documented, time-boxed business decision* — then *fix the process* (the *SBOM* is a *PR gate*, so the *next* *vuln* is *caught early*, not a *big-bang block*)."

**S10. The *CTO* asks: "Is our *platform* a *product*, or a *cost center*? Prove it in 2 minutes."**
